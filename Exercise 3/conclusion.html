What This Means for You

After exploring six visualisations of the Australian TV energy dataset, here are the most important takeaways for consumers, policy makers, and researchers.

📐

Screen Size is the Biggest Driver

Size matters more than any other factor. Choosing a 55" TV over an 85" can cut power use by nearly 50%. If energy costs are a concern, choose the smallest screen that meets your needs.

🖥️

OLED is More Efficient at the Same Size

At equivalent screen sizes, OLED models consistently draw less power than LCD (LED) equivalents. If you want a large screen, an OLED may actually be the greener choice compared to a similarly sized LCD (LED).

📊

LCD (LED) Dominates the Market

Over 80% of models use LCD (LED) technology, giving consumers the widest choice of brands, sizes, and price points. Competition in this segment keeps prices low but also drives rapid efficiency improvements.

⭐

Interpret Star Ratings in Context

A 3.5-star 85" TV may be highly efficient for its class. Don't reject a large TV solely based on its star rating — compare within the same size category. Aim for 5+ stars for screens under 55".

🏷️

Brand Matters Less Than You Think

Budget brands like Hisense, TCL, and Kogan offer comparable efficiency ratings to premium brands at significantly lower prices. The energy label is a better guide than brand reputation.

🌱

Small Choices Add Up

A TV running 4 hours per day, 365 days a year draws between ~100 kWh (32" LCD) and ~470 kWh (85" LCD LED) annually. At Australian electricity rates, that's a $20–$95 annual difference — just from choosing the right screen size.
